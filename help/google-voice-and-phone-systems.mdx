Internet phone services and office phone systems forward calls through settings, not dial codes.
Most work well.
A few add steps that an agent cannot complete, such as asking the person who answers to press a key.
This page covers those cases.

## Google Voice

Google states that forwarding calls from a Google Voice number to an automated system is unsupported.
A Larynth agent is an automated system.
So forwarding from a personal Google Voice number may not work, and it can stop working without notice.

What this means for you:

- Do not rely on a personal Google Voice number as the only way callers reach your agent.
- Google points to Google Voice for Google Workspace, which has an auto attendant, for forwarding to automated systems.
- You can give callers your Larynth number directly.
- You can forward from a different phone service that supports it.

If you try it anyway, two Google Voice limits apply.
You can link up to 6 numbers.
You cannot link a number that is already linked to another Google Voice number.

Also turn off call screening, described below.

## "Press 1 to accept" prompts

Some phone services screen forwarded calls.
When the forwarded call is answered, the service plays a prompt such as "press 1 to accept this call".
A person can press 1.
An agent cannot, so the call never connects and the caller usually ends up in voicemail.

Turn the prompt off for the line you forward.

| Service | Setting | What to do |
| - | - | - |
| Google Voice | **Screen Calls** | In Google Voice, open **Settings**, then **Calls**, and turn **Screen Calls** off. |
| RingCentral | **Incoming call information** | In the Admin Portal, open **My Extension**, then **Settings**, then **Phone**, then **Incoming call information**. Under **Play announcement**, choose **Never**. |
| Zoom Phone | **Require to press 1 before connecting the call** | In the Zoom web portal, open **Zoom Phone**, then **Settings**. Under **Business Hours** or **Closed Hours**, select **Edit** next to **Call Handling**. Clear the checkbox for the forwarding number. |

Notes:

- RingCentral says to choose **Never** when calls are forwarded to an answering service.
- Zoom turns this checkbox on by default.
- Zoom says it cannot be turned off for call queues, so forward from a user's line, not a queue.
- Menu names change over time, so search your service's help for "call screening" if you cannot find a setting.

If your service is not listed, ask its support whether forwarded calls to an outside number need the answering party to press a key.
If that cannot be turned off, the service cannot forward to an agent.

## Other internet phone services and office systems

The steps are similar everywhere.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the settings for your business number">
    Sign in to your phone service.
    Find **Call forwarding**, **Call handling** or **Call routing**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your Larynth number as an outside number">
    Enter the full number, including +1.
    Choose every call, or only calls nobody answers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check that outside forwarding is allowed">
    Some services block forwarding to outside numbers until an administrator allows it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save and test">
    Call your business number from a different phone.
    The agent should answer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The dashboard has the same steps with your number filled in.
Open **Phone numbers**, open your number, and next to **Forward your existing number** select **Open**.
Choose **An internet phone service** or **An office phone system**.

If someone else manages your office phones, send them this page.

## Keep the caller's number

Larynth shows the caller's number when your phone service passes it along.
Some services replace it with your own business number on forwarded calls.
Then every call looks like it came from you.

If your service has a caller ID option for forwarded calls, set it to show the caller.
On RingCentral this is **Incoming caller ID**, in the same **Incoming call information** window.

## Do not transfer back to the number you forward from

Your agent can transfer a caller to a person.
Do not use the number you forward from as the transfer number.

Here is what happens if you do:

1. A caller dials your business number.
2. Your phone company forwards the call to your agent.
3. The agent transfers the call to your business number.
4. Your phone company forwards it to the agent again.

The caller goes around in a loop and never reaches a person.

Use a different line for transfers, such as a direct mobile number that is not forwarded.
The same applies to the **Transfer to** number under **If the agent can't pick up** on a number's routing.

When a transfer reaches you, your phone shows your Larynth number, not the caller's.
You'll hear who's calling and why before you're connected.

See [Handoff to a person](/concepts/handoff) for how transfers work.
